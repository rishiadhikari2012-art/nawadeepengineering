Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions about your building drawing, Naksa pass, structural design, or property valuation? Here are quick answers.

To apply for a Municipal Naksa Pass (Building Permit), you generally need: 1. Copy of Land Ownership Certificate (Lalpurja), 2. Cadastral Map (Trace Naksa) and Blue Print from the Land Survey (Napi) Office, 3. Citizenship Certificate of the landowner, 4. Recent Land Revenue Tax Receipt (Tiro Tireko Rasid), 5. Road access recommendation letter (Bato Sifaris) if needed, and 6. Complete architectural, structural, sanitary, and electrical drawings prepared by an authorized engineering consultancy like Nawadeep Engineering Services.

Nepal is located in a seismically active zone. Architectural drawings define the room layout, but structural design mathematically calculates the exact size of columns, beams, foundation depth, and steel rebar required to withstand major earthquake forces without structural failure. Proper structural design also prevents over-reinforcement, saving substantial construction costs.

Initial 2D conceptual floor plans are typically ready within 2 to 4 working days after the site survey and client brief. Once floor plans are approved, 3D exterior elevation renders and structural analysis take an additional 3 to 7 working days.

When applying for a home loan, commercial mortgage, or bank credit line, commercial banks require an official Property Valuation Report prepared by an authorized engineer to assess the Fair Market Value (FMV) and Distress Value (DV) of the land and building.
